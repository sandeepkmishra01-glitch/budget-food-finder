## Design Context

### Users
General budget-conscious food seekers — families, tourists, locals, anyone who wants good food without overpaying. Used on phones and desktops, often when hungry and deciding where to eat. Context is immediate: "I'm here, I'm hungry, what's good and cheap nearby?"

### Brand Personality
**Curated, confident, appetizing** — like a well-designed food magazine that respects your wallet. Not cheap-looking despite being about cheap food. The design should make $7 tacos feel like a discovery, not a compromise.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Visual tone**: Clean food magazine — editorial layouts, generous whitespace, food photography as the hero. Refined but approachable.
- **Theme**: Warm light — creamy backgrounds, not sterile white. Feels like a café menu or artisan food zine printed on unbleached stock.
- **Color palette**: Bold foodie colors — deep reds (paprika, tomato), golden yellows (turmeric, honey), rich greens (herb, olive). Colors of actual ingredients and spices. Used as accents against warm neutrals.
- **Anti-references**: Generic Yelp/DoorDash blue-and-white utility UI. No corporate tech feel. No cold grays.
- **References**: Bon Appétit magazine layouts, The Infatuation's editorial voice, Eater's bold typography.

### Design Principles
1. **Food is the hero** — Large, beautiful food imagery dominates. UI chrome is minimal and warm-toned.
2. **Editorial confidence** — Bold type choices, clear hierarchy, decisive layout. Not tentative or cluttered.
3. **Warm materiality** — Feels like printed paper, not a screen. Subtle textures, warm tones, natural feeling.
4. **Instant clarity** — Despite the editorial feel, information is scannable. Price, distance, rating visible at a glance.
5. **Budget without compromise** — The design elevates affordable food. Never feels "discount" or cheap.
